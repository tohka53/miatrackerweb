5. Cookies and similar technologies

A cookie is a small file the site stores in your browser so it can remember something between one page and the next. We use three kinds:

Strictly necessary cookies

These make the site work: remembering your language preference, remembering that you already dismissed the cookie notice, and protecting forms against automated submissions. They cannot be turned off without breaking the site and do not require your consent.

Analytics cookies

These tell us how many people visit each page and how they move through the site, in aggregate. We use them to learn which content works. You can refuse them without losing any functionality.

Marketing cookies

These let us measure how our campaigns perform and show relevant ads on other platforms. They are only set if you accept them.

How to control them

You can change your choice at any time from the cookie notice on the site, or block and delete cookies from your browser settings (Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge all allow this under privacy settings). If you block everything, some parts of the site may not behave as expected.

We also honor Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals sent by your browser as an opt-out of analytics and marketing cookies.

9. How we protect information

We maintain a written information security program with administrative, technical and physical safeguards, as required of anyone holding personal information about Massachusetts residents (201 CMR 17.00). In practice that means:

  • HTTPS/TLS encryption in transit across the whole site.
  • Access limited to the people who need it to do their job.
  • Multi-factor authentication on the tools where contact records live.
  • Regular backups and patching of the site's components.

No system connected to the internet is invulnerable. If a security incident materially affects your personal information, we will notify you and the appropriate authorities as required by Massachusetts breach notification law (M.G.L. c. 93H) and any other law that applies to you, together with what we are doing about it.

10. Your privacy rights

We extend the following rights to every visitor, not only to residents of states that already mandate them:

  • Know and access: find out what information we hold about you and what we do with it.
  • Correct: fix information that is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Delete: have your information erased when we no longer need it.

13. Governing law

This policy is governed by the laws of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts and the applicable federal laws of the United States. There is no single comprehensive federal privacy statute in the U.S.; instead, the following apply to a site like ours:

  • 201 CMR 17.00 — Massachusetts standards for protecting the personal information of Commonwealth residents, which require a written information security program.
  • M.G.L. c. 93H and c. 93I — Massachusetts data breach notification and secure destruction of records containing personal information.
  • CAN-SPAM Act — rules for commercial email, including a working unsubscribe mechanism.
  • COPPA — protection of children under 13 online.
  • FTC Act, Section 5 — which makes this policy enforceable: we have to do what it says.

Massachusetts does not yet have a comprehensive consumer privacy statute. A Massachusetts Data Privacy Act has passed both chambers of the legislature in separate versions and is being reconciled as of the date of this policy. We have written the rights in section 10 to meet what that bill and comparable state laws — such as the California Consumer Privacy Act — would require, so nothing about how we treat you needs to change when one of them takes effect.

If you are a resident of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia or another state with a comprehensive privacy law, exercise your rights through section 10 and tell us which state you are in; we will apply whatever that law adds. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, the same channel applies, without prejudice to your right to complain to your national supervisory authority.
